Nine years, one architecture

How we got here

This entire field is younger than most degrees in this room. Nine years from one paper to software that reads a customer's photograph.

The most useful thing on the previous slide

ChatGPT was not a technical breakthrough

What existed already
GPT-3 had been available through an API since 2020. Developers had been building on it for two years. The capability was not the news.
What was actually new
A chat box. Free access. A conversation that remembered the previous turn. In other words — a product.
Why that decides what we teach
Nobody in this room is going to train a frontier model, and nobody needs to. The value was created in the layer wrapped around the model — the interface, the memory, the access to real data, the guardrails, the human approval step. That layer is what these twenty sessions are about.

It is also why we teach architecture rather than a model. Every model ID in tonight's notebook will eventually be retired. The shape of the application will not.

The map of the field

One question splits the whole industry

Can you download the weights?

Closed weights — API only
OpenAI · GPTAnthropic · Claude Google · GeminixAI · Grok

You get an endpoint and a bill. Frontier capability, zero ops burden — and your data leaves your building, while prices and models change under you.
Open weights — download and run
Meta · LlamaMistral Alibaba · QwenDeepSeekGoogle · Gemma

You get files you can run on your own hardware. Privacy, cost control, no lock-in, fine-tuning — and you now own the GPUs, the latency, the uptime and the upgrades.
"Open weights" is not "open source"
You get the trained parameters, usually under a licence with conditions. You very rarely get the training data or the code that produced them. It is closer to a free binary than to open-source software — read the licence before you build a business on one.

When it genuinely decides the architecture: a hospital or a bank in Dhaka that cannot send patient or account data to a US API does not have a "which model scores higher" problem. It has a must run on our own hardware requirement — and that settles the question before any benchmark is opened.

Every model launches with a chart showing it winning

How to read a leaderboard without being fooled

arena.ai/leaderboard
How the arena works
You type a prompt. Two anonymous models answer. You pick the better one — and only then are you told which was which. Millions of blind votes become an Elo rating, the same system that ranks chess players. Nobody can train for it, because the questions are whatever real people happened to ask.
Why that beats a vendor's own chart
Self-reported benchmarks are marketing, and benchmark questions leak into training data. A blind human vote on a fresh question is the hardest signal to game — which is exactly why it is the one worth showing you.

Three caveats, and they are the actual skill

CaveatWhat to do about it
It measures preference, not correctnessA confident, well-formatted, slightly wrong answer beats a hedged correct one — because that's what people click.
First place is usually noiseThe top several are typically inside each other's error bars. "Top five" is signal; "#1 this week" mostly isn't.
Overall rank answers no real questionUse the category boards — coding, maths, long context, vision. They disagree with each other, and one of them is your job.

Also worth bookmarking: artificialanalysis.ai, which plots quality against price and speed. For ShopWise the honest question is never "which model is best" — it's "which is good enough at this task for the least money".

The other half of the market

Someone will run the open models for you

Downloading Llama does not mean you want to operate Llama. Running open weights well needs GPUs, batching, autoscaling, and someone awake at 3am. So an entire industry exists to run them for you, behind an API that mostly imitates OpenAI's.

ProviderKnown for
Groq · groq.comCustom hardware — open models at remarkable speed
Together AI · together.aiBroad catalogue of open models, plus fine-tuning
Fireworks · ReplicateFast hosted inference; image and video models too
OpenRouter · openrouter.aiOne key, one endpoint, in front of hundreds of models — closed and open — with live per-token prices
Why this matters to what we're building
It means the model is a replaceable component. Before you paste a key into anything

Two things about the free tier

⚠️ Free-tier prompts are used to train Google's models
Their pricing page lists "content used to improve our products" as Yes for free and No for paid. So: never paste real customer data, real names, card numbers, or anything belonging to your employer into a free-tier notebook. Every ticket in data/tickets.yml is invented precisely so this course needs nothing real.

This is also an interview question. "How do you handle data governance with a third-party model?" has a right answer, and it starts here.
Rate limits are no longer published
Google removed the requests-per-minute table; the docs now say limits "can be viewed in Google AI Studio". So distrust any number in any blog post — including any number I might put on a slide. Check your own, together, now:

ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits · aistudio.google.com/rate-limit

When a cell returns 429 RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED, that's a rate limit — not a bug in your code. Wait a minute, re-run.
